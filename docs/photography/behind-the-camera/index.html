Behind the Camera

How my journey into photography started, the photographers who inspire me, and the equipment I carry on my trips.

How It Started

I’ve always liked taking pictures. As a kid, I was the one in charge of taking the family photos, trying to get them sharp, not blurry, and memorable. This carried on with the arrival of smartphones, where I took pictures from time to time.

Around the beginning of 2017, I thought it was time to treat photography as an art, not just a source of random memories. I was about to leave for Seattle for a month, and that was when I decided to jump. I bought a mirrorless camera and a tripod a few days before my departure, and I discovered a new world: choosing a proper subject, choosing the time of day, learning how to adjust the exposure, and how to capture a scene with a lot of dynamic range.

As I was living in London at the time, I continued my journey and focused mainly on cityscapes, trying to capture some of the most beautiful monuments of the UK. After five years abroad, I decided to come back to France, my home country.

That is when I turned to landscapes. The lavender fields of Valensole or the seascapes of the West Coast were, and still are, absolute treasures to my eyes. From time to time I travel beyond my comfort zone, to Spain, Finland, or Switzerland, to see a different but equally gorgeous nature, and I keep going back to the UK. Still, I keep being drawn back to the landscapes of Normandy and Brittany.

My Inspirations

Over the years, I’ve been inspired by several photographers. To name a few: Elia Locardi, Daniel Kordan, Aaron Dowling, Thomas Heaton, Nick Page, and Albert Dros. But the artist who inspired me the most at the time was probably Marc Adamus.

Now I feel I’m at a turning point with this hobby. I have the feeling I need to try something different, less tailored to social media algorithms, less epic, probably more minimalist. More time outdoors, less time in post-processing. After talking with photographers like Jean-Luc Girod and Ian Lawson, I realized pictures were more than a good composition with plenty of colors. They could be moments in time, with emotions and stories to tell.

So I’m trying to change my approach. Like many before me, I’m studying the pictures of Ansel Adams and Galen Rowell, who are now a deep inspiration. Artists like Art Wolfe, Jimmy Chin, and Krystle Wright are the examples I’m trying to follow today.

What I Carry

Since day one, I’ve used Fujifilm equipment. I’m the lucky owner of an X-T2. I shoot mostly with the XF 10–24 mm f/4, I never got tired of the XF 18–55 mm f/2.8–4, and I acquired an XF 70–300 mm f/4–5.6 for very isolated landscapes. My Gitzo GK1545T tripod has followed me around the world without a single issue. A few years ago I switched to Really Right Stuff ball heads, such as the BH-40, and I’m still exploring all their possibilities.

Say Hi

I do have an Instagram account, even though I’m a very infrequent user. The galleries on this website contain the pieces of my work I found the most interesting to share, but for the most curious, additional pictures can be found there. Either way, feel free to say hi!
